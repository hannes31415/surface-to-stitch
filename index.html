surface-to-stitch

Crochet patterns for surfaces of revolution. Give it a profile z = f(r); it walks the curve at equal arc length, converts each radius into a whole number of stitches, and spaces the increases and decreases so they never stack up in a seam.

How the placement works

Loops sit at equal arc length along the profile, not at equal height, so the fabric keeps an even row gauge as the surface steepens. Loop n lands at the radius where the arc from the centre is exactly n/N of the total.

Where consecutive loops need j and k shaping stitches, the second ring is rotated by π / lcm(j, k). That is the offset that keeps increases from landing on top of each other loop after loop, which is what produces visible ridges instead of a smooth curve.
